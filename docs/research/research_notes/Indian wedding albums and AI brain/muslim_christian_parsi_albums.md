## Indian Muslim weddings: events, must-have moments, separate men's/women's areas, and whether the bride is photographed with the groom at the nikah

### Takeaway
The common North Indian order is manjha/ubtan → mehndi → sanchaq → baraat → nikah (ijab-qubool, the contract signed, mehr) → arsi mushaf (couple's first look in a mirror, Quran between them) → joota chupai → rukhsati → walima (groom's side, days later) → chauthi. In many families the bride sits in a separate room with the women during the nikah, and her consent is carried to the men by a representative. So the nikah often gives two separate photo sets (the groom with the qazi and the men, and the bride with the women), plus a first couple photo only after the nikah (veil lift or mirror). Women's areas are often covered by a female photographer. Regional forms (Kerala Mappila, Bengali, Kashmiri, Bohra) change the names and add events, but the nikah, the farewell and the reception stay central.

### Cited Findings
**General order (North Indian / Lucknowi pattern)**
- Manjha: the turmeric, sandalwood and rose-water ceremony, about two days before the wedding (some sources say 3-5 days). — [MuslimKnot](https://www.muslimknot.com/blog/traditions-and-ceremonies-of-an-indian-muslim-wedding); [CulturalIndia](https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/regional-weddings/muslim-wedding.html)
- Manjha: the groom's family sends the clothes and ornaments the bride wears at the nikah. — [Wowdings (Delhi nikaah blog)](https://wowdings.com/nikaah-muslim-wedding-photography-blog-in-delhi/amp)
- Sanchaq: often called the final pre-wedding ritual, 1-2 days before the nikah. The groom's family brings gifts, the bridal outfit, jewellery and cosmetics. — [OYO blog](https://www.oyorooms.com/blog/13-muslim-wedding-rituals-ceremonies-know/); [getethnic](https://getethnic.com/blogs/muslim-wedding/)
- Joota chupai: the bride's sisters or friends steal the groom's shoes and ask a ransom. Sources disagree on when it happens: some put it on the wedding day, others with arsi mushaf just before rukhsati. — [Symphony Events](https://symphonyevents.com.au/muslim-wedding/); [Outspoken Visions](https://outspokenvisions.com/indian-muslim-wedding/)
- Walima: the groom's family's reception. Some sources say it comes about 5 days to a week after rukhsati (others say the next day or within 3 days, see the Bengali section). Chauthi: on the fourth day the bride visits her parents' home with her husband. — [OYO blog](https://www.oyorooms.com/blog/13-muslim-wedding-rituals-ceremonies-know/); [CulturalIndia](https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/regional-weddings/muslim-wedding.html)
- The whole nikah ritual is called ijab-e-qabool. The walis (fathers) or brothers of both bride and groom are present. — [Fine Art Production: Muslim wedding traditions](https://fineartproduction.com/muslim-wedding-traditions)
- Arsi mushaf: the couple may look at each other through a mirror placed between them, with a copy of the Quran between their seats; one vendor says the maulvi then guides them in prayers. The spelling (Arsi Mushaf / Musaf / Musharraf) and whether the Quran or the mirror comes first both vary. — [Fine Art Production](https://www.fineartproduction.com/muslim-wedding-photography); [Bollywood Shaadis](https://mirror2stevejobs.bollywoodshaadis.com/articles/sacred-rituals-of-an-indian-muslim-wedding-1825)
- Rukhsati: the bride leaves her family home for her husband's. It is described as the emotional end of the wedding, "a mix of happiness and sadness". — [Visual Artistry Co: Nikah to Rukhsati](https://www.visualartistry.co/blog-1/69ggwzljs7gffkl-nyj5k-eglnj-yh5rh-j97w4-pxeaz-rl8xh-7zx8k-zljrs-2hddk-3blnt-d9p5r-89nxr-d7nnd-chbgt-xhm8f-tjd5d-3wfpk-fn4cc-49c64-s8p2t-z93gf); [Wowdings](https://wowdings.com/nikaah-a-muslim-wedding-photography-blog/)
- "Customs vary by country and region, but at its heart the nikah ceremony stays the same." — [Fine Art Production](https://www.fineartproduction.com/muslim-wedding-photography)

**Nikah photography: what to capture and how**
- The nikah is "solemn and dignified". Photographers are told to be respectful and unobtrusive and to take candid shots of the vows and the contract signing. Bride and groom may sit apart, with consent taken from each in front of witnesses. — [Visual Artistry Co: photographer's guide to Muslim rituals](https://www.visualartistry.co/blog-1/69ggwzljs7gffkl-nyj5k)
- Advice: watch the nikah from the sidelines so you don't disturb it, and follow the family's wishes on sharing photos, especially on social media. — [Visual Artistry Co: key tips](https://visualartistry.co/blog-1/69ggwzljs7gffkl-nyj5k-eglnj-yh5rh-j97w4-pxeaz-rl8xh-7zx8k-zljrs-2hddk-3blnt)
- A Bangalore studio lists, among its usual nikah shots, the moment the groom lifts the bride's veil and looks at her after the nikah. — [One Horizon Productions](https://onehorizonproductions.com/muslim-wedding-photography/)
- A nikah shot list (Malaysian "akad nikah" context, not India) covers the groom's arrival, his parents' proud faces, a close-up of the rings, and the couple's first look at each other. — [My Wedding Edit](https://myweddingedit.com/11-must-have-shots-for-your-akad/)
- Photographers recommend making the shot list with the couple, covering the key moments and the group photos they want. — [Visual Artistry Co](https://visualartistry.co/blog-1/69ggwzljs7gffkl-nyj5k-eglnj-yh5rh-j97w4-pxeaz-rl8xh-7zx8k-zljrs-2hddk-3blnt)

**Separate men's/women's areas, purdah, female photographers**

**Kerala Mappila (Malabar)**
- The nikah is held in the bride's family home, a mosque, or (for more modern families) the reception venue. One source says that at the mosque it happens "in the presence of the bride and the groom's father and other elderly relatives". — [Kerala Tourism](https://www.keralatourism.org/islam-in-kerala/culture/start-of-marriage); [Malabar Gold: Kerala Muslim rituals](https://www.malabargoldanddiamonds.com/brides-of-india/traditional-bride-61/south-indian-bride/kerala-muslim-bridal-jewellery/rituals.html)
- Bride and groom sit apart. One source says their heads are covered with a cloth so they cannot see each other. Rituals involving the bride take place in a separate part of the house. — [panigrahana.com](https://panigrahana.com/muslim-wedding-kerala-malabar.html); [Malabar Gold](https://www.malabargoldanddiamonds.com/brides-of-india/traditional-bride-61/south-indian-bride/kerala-muslim-bridal-jewellery/rituals.html)
- Gold is central: mehr can be gold, cash or property, and the nikah outfit is red-and-gold with heavy gold jewellery. One vendor puts a full Malabar bridal set at 80-200 g. — [itsmy.wedding: Malabar nikah](https://itsmy.wedding/blog/malabar-muslim-nikah-guide); [itsmy.wedding: bridal look](https://itsmy.wedding/blog/kerala-muslim-bridal-look)
- Oppana (women's song-and-clap performance around the bride) is part of Mappila weddings and usually comes just before the groom leaves for the bride's home, where the nikah takes place. The thali-tying ritual is rare in Mappila weddings because it is seen as contrary to Islamic custom. — [Wikipedia: Oppana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppana); [Malabar Gold](https://www.malabargoldanddiamonds.com/us/brides-of-india/traditional-bride/south-indian-bride/kerala-muslim-bridal-jewellery/traditions.html)
- The Malabar wedding is usually described as a three-day celebration around the nikah, performed by a qazi. — [panigrahana.com](https://panigrahana.com/muslim-wedding-kerala-malabar.html)

**Bengali Muslim**
- Gaye holud (turmeric) is held separately at the bride's home and the groom's home. It came from Hindu Bengali practice and is still central. — [Wikipedia: Gaye holud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaye_holud); [Wikipedia: Bengali Muslim wedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslim_wedding)
- Bou bhaat is the groom's family's reception, usually the day after the wedding, with "bhaat kapor" (a sari on a plate given to the bride). One listing puts the walima within three days of the nikah, with the bidai after the nikah. The Wikipedia article is marked incomplete. — [Wikipedia: Bengali Muslim wedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslim_wedding); [Symphony Events: Bangladeshi wedding](https://symphonyevents.com.au/bangladeshi-wedding/)

**Kashmiri Muslim**
- Wanvun: women's folk singing on evenings at both homes in the days before the wedding. — [Wikipedia: Wanvun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanvun); [CulturalIndia: Kashmiri](https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/regional-weddings/kashmiri-wedding.html)
- Maenziraat (mehndi night) falls a day or two before the wedding (one source says a week). It is followed by the nikah under a qazi with Quran recitation, the wazwan feast, and rukhsati. — [CulturalIndia](https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/regional-weddings/kashmiri-wedding.html); [Plannersy](https://plannersy.com/wedding/kashmiri-wedding-rituals/); [weddingsonline.in](https://www.weddingsonline.in/blog/charming-wedding-rituals-of-kashmir/)

**Dawoodi Bohra**
- At a Mumbai mass wedding, the brides sat apart and each nikah was made between the groom and the bride's father, in front of the Da'i al-Mutlaq. The photographer said it took a while to get permission to shoot. — [SILK / Sephi Bergerson](https://www.silkphotos.com/wedding-photography/mumbai/dawoodi-bhora-mass-wedding/); [sephibergerson.com](https://www.sephibergerson.com/indian-wedding-traditions/dawoodi-bhora-mass-wedding-in-mumbai/)
- Rasm-e-Saifee community mass weddings: 20 couples in Udaipur, with a baraat procession of 45 grooms on horses. — [Udaipur Times](https://udaipurtimes.com/events/photos-mass-procession-organized-by-bohra-community/c74416-w2859-cid117498-s10700.htm)
- Misaaq (oath of allegiance) is reported as a requirement before a Bohra nikah in some cases (a personal account). — [dawoodi-bohras.com](https://www.dawoodi-bohras.com/news/19/71/A-wedding-ordeal)

**Hyderabadi**
- Hyderabadi weddings run about five days, with dholak ke geet (women's dholak songs). The bride wears a khara dupatta. No Hyderabad-specific order of events was found. — [Wikipedia: Dholak ke Geet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholak_ke_Geet); [getethnic](https://getethnic.com/blogs/muslim-wedding/)

### Inferences
- For the app, a Muslim nikah folder may contain many photos of the groom with the qazi and the men, and separately the bride with women, with no couple frame until the mirror or veil-lift moment. The couple detector should not expect the couple "often together" inside the nikah event. The first couple frame (mirror, veil lift, joint seating after the nikah) is likely the most precious couple photo of the day.
- A Quran, a mirror, the nikahnama being signed, and the mehr gold are "detail" shots with meaning. Signing photos often show a man's hand and paper with no face visible, so they would count as face-less detail shots.
- Bride-only photos with many women (purdah area, mehndi, oppana, dholak) are core content, not leftovers. Women-only events may come from a separate female photographer's sub-folder.
- Rukhsati tears need the same "tears are never a flaw" exception as the Hindu vidaai. The walima/bou bhaat often comes days later and may be its own sub-folder, with stage photos with families.
- Some families do not want women's photos shared. The app's "never upload" promise matches this, and sending women-area photos only to the bride's family copy may matter.

### Gaps
- No source found on photography rules inside Indian mosques (whether cameras are allowed, men-only prayer halls).
- No India-specific source on how many nikah photos are printed, or on the album page order photographers use for Muslim albums.
- Konkani Muslim (Navayath/Bhatkal) and Lucknowi-specific rituals: nothing found. Hyderabadi order not confirmed.
- No source quantifies how common the separate-room nikah is versus the couple seated together (the second is common in modern urban weddings, but no source was found).

## Christian weddings (Kerala Syrian, Goan, Mangalorean, Tamil/Andhra, North-East): events and must-have shots

### Takeaway
All Indian Christian albums share a church core: the groom waiting at the altar, the bride's walk down the aisle (often with her father), readings and choir, vows, ring blessing and exchange, signing the register with witnesses, then the exit and portraits at the church, and the evening reception (cake, toast, first dance, band). Each community adds its own anchor moments: Kerala minnu kettu and manthrakodi (plus crowning for Orthodox/Jacobite); the Goan/Mangalorean roce (coconut milk and oil anointing, messy and fun) and, for Mangaloreans, the opsun farewell; Mizo church weddings with white gowns and a community feast. The kiss is listed in some studio shot lists, but no Indian source confirms it is common.

### Cited Findings
**Church ceremony shot list (Indian studios)**
- A Ranchi studio's list, in order: the groom waiting at the altar, the procession, readings and choir, vows and rings (two angles), the first kiss, and signing the register with witnesses. Then the exit with petals or confetti, 20-30 minutes of couple portraits at the church (doors, greenery), and family groups. — [Subodh Multimedia shot list](https://www.subodhmultimedia.com/blog/christian-wedding-photography-shot-list-from-the-church-ceremony-to-the-reception/)
- A Chennai studio adds the groom getting his parents' blessing before the ceremony, the bridal party just before the procession, the giving away, worship and readings, the vows, and signing the register. A few close-ups and couple photos come before the reception. — [HiQ Weddings](https://www.hiqweddings.net/blog/christian-wedding-photography-chennai)
- Vendor coverage of the reception usually lists "candid and portrait coverage of the reception, toasts and first dance". — [Click Factory Photography](https://clickfactoryphotography.com/christian-wedding-photography/); [REM Video & Photography](https://www.remvp.com/blog-post/indian-christian-wedding-traditions)
- After the service, families and guests move to a banquet hall for the reception. — [WeddingBazaar](https://www.weddingbazaar.com/blog/indian-christian-wedding-rituals)

**Kerala Syrian Christian (Catholic, Orthodox, Jacobite, Marthoma, Knanaya)**
- Madhuram veppu ("placing the sweet") is held separately at both homes on the evening before the wedding. Margamkali dance is common at it in Syrian Catholic and Knanaya families. At the groom's home, the barber shaving the groom is part of the eve rituals (madhuramvekkal). — [velvetknot](https://velvetknot.in/syrian-christian-wedding-traditions/); [bigfatweddingsite](https://bigfatweddingsite.wordpress.com/2017/01/07/the-mallu-syrian-christian-rituals/)
- Mylanchi (henna): one guide says it is Knanaya only and not done in Syrian Catholic or Marthoma families. Another says a mehendi like the Muslim one is now often held with madhuram veppu. The sources disagree. — [velvetknot](https://velvetknot.in/syrian-christian-wedding-traditions/); [bigfatweddingsite](https://bigfatweddingsite.wordpress.com/2017/01/07/the-mallu-syrian-christian-rituals/)
- Church rite: consent; rings blessed and exchanged (if not done at the betrothal); minnu blessed and tied by the groom; manthrakodi draped; the nuptial blessing. Orthodox and Jacobite families add the crowning. — [itsmy.wedding: Kerala Christian rituals](https://itsmy.wedding/blog/christian-wedding-rituals-kerala); [itsmy.wedding: Syrian Christian planning](https://itsmy.wedding/blog/syrian-christian-wedding-planning-kerala)
- Minnu-kettu: the bride kneels facing the altar beside the groom. The priest blesses the manthrakodi and hands it to the groom, who drapes it over the bride's head. The minnu hangs on a string of seven threads taken from the manthrakodi, and is moved to a gold chain days later. — [ktexplorer](https://ktexplorer.com/post/christian-weddings); [Fine Art Production: Malayalee Christian](https://www.fineartproduction.com/malyalee-christian-wedding-tradition)
- Minnu shape is reported as a heart or a banyan leaf, often with a cross. One account says the groom's sister threads it. — [Malabar Gold: Kerala Christian rituals](https://malabargoldanddiamonds.com/ae/brides-of-india/traditional-bride/south-indian-bride/kerala-christian-bridal-jewellery/rituals.html); [itsmy.wedding](https://itsmy.wedding/blog/christian-wedding-rituals-kerala)
- Manthrakodi: a sari from the groom's family, blessed by the priest. Traditionally off-white or cream with a gold kasavu border; others say red. Some accounts say the bride changes into it for the feast. — [itsmy.wedding](https://itsmy.wedding/blog/christian-wedding-rituals-kerala); [jacobshorizon](https://jacobshorizon.blogspot.com/2019/04/beautiful-amalgamation-of-hindu-and.html)
- One account says minnu kettu, manthrakodi and kanyadanam are done in the church after the purely Christian rites, as they are not part of the liturgy. — [jacobshorizon](https://jacobshorizon.blogspot.com/2019/04/beautiful-amalgamation-of-hindu-and.html)
- A grand feast follows the church rite. — [velvetknot](https://velvetknot.in/syrian-christian-wedding-traditions/)
- A Kerala studio: "The photograph you'll treasure most won't be the posed portrait" (it stresses candids). — [Imagix Films Studio](https://imagixfilmsstudio.blogspot.com/2026/05/wedding-photography-kerala-where-every.html)

**Goan Catholic**
- Roce: on the eve (or 2-3 days before), family members apply fresh coconut milk and coconut oil to the bride and groom as a blessing and purification. It is messy and lively ("a lot of fun"). Some families hold a church blessing first, and the decor includes crosses, statues of Our Lady and candles. — [Aza Fashions: Roce](https://www.azafashions.com/blog/roce-ceremony-goan-wedding-rituals-meaning-2026/); [Pernia's: Goa](https://www.perniaspopupshop.com/wedding-encyclopedia/goa); [Museum of Goa](https://museumofgoa.com/art-project/where-portugal-met-konkan-the-extraordinary-traditions-of-goan-catholic-weddings/)
- Church: the priest reads from the Bible, hymns and prayers; the couple say their vows and are pronounced man and wife. Most have a "white wedding": brides in white, women in gowns, men including the groom in western suits. — [Wikipedia: Culture of Goan Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Goan_Catholics); [Utsavpedia](https://www.utsavpedia.com/weddings-festivals/going-to-a-goan-wedding/)
- The reception has ballroom dancing and a live western band as "an integral part". The xim (boundary) ceremony with poured liquor usually comes after the reception. — [Goan Observer](https://www.goanobserver.in/2022/04/08/unique-wedding-traditions-of-goan-catholics/); [goaholidayhomes](https://www.goaholidayhomes.com/information/the-goan-catholic-wedding.html)

**Mangalorean Catholic**
- Vojem: relatives bring food gifts (ash pumpkin, bottle gourd, a moora of rice), now often only as a token. — [konkanisunited](http://konkanisunited.blogspot.com/2016/02/the-roce-ceremony.html); [GOYA](https://www.goya.in/blog/foods-that-mark-the-mangalore-catholic-circle-of-life)
- Roce: it starts with "voviyo" songs (Konkani blessing songs, sung by women). Guests, parents first, make the sign of the cross on the forehead with coconut milk and oil. After the groom's roce, he is led by the hand to bathe. The timing varies (bride's on the eve and groom's on the morning of the wedding, or 1-3 days before). — [cooklikececilia](https://cooklikececilia.com/2018/05/29/mangalorean-marriage-customs-and-traditions-roce/); [mangaluruonline](https://www.mangaluruonline.in/guide/magalorean-catholic-wedding)
- At the end of the reception comes the opsun farewell (like the Hindu vidaai), when the bride's parents symbolically hand their daughter over to her in-laws. — [Saral Marriage](https://blog.saralmarriage.com/sacred-rituals-mangalorean-catholic-wedding/)

**North-East (Mizo, Naga, Khasi)**
- Most Mizos are Christian and marry in church. A bride-price agreement is a prerequisite, and the puanchei cloth matters. — [Government of Mizoram](http://mizoram.nic.in/about/custom.htm)
- "Almost every Mizo bride who gets married in a church dons a beautiful white gown." A feast follows at the bride's home or a community hall. — [Pernia's: Mizoram](https://www.perniaspopupshop.com/wedding-encyclopedia/mizoram/wedding); [Utsavpedia: Mizoram](https://www.utsavpedia.com/weddings-festivals/unique-in-every-way-mizoram-wedding/)
- Khasi (matrilineal): the man moves to the wife's home. Naga customs are reported alongside the church service. These are blog claims only. — [weddingz.in](https://weddingz.in/blog/10-beautiful-north-east-wedding-rituals/); [velvetknot](https://velvetknot.in/christian-wedding-traditions-india/)

### Inferences
- The church is the "big moment" event for these albums. Its order is fixed and easy to recognise in time: aisle walk → vows/rings → (minnu/manthrakodi) → register → exit. One good photo of each step matters more than many near-identical altar frames.
- Many church photos are taken from behind (couple facing the altar) or at the kneeling bride. "Backs of guests" and "not looking at the camera" rules must not drop these. The aisle walk and the father giving the bride away are must-haves even if faces are side-on.
- The roce is like the haldi: messy faces covered in coconut milk are a joy, not a "face blocked" flaw.
- The reception adds western moments (cake, toast, first dance, band). These are often dim with coloured light, the same issue as the sangeet.
- Kiss photos: some Indian studio lists include the kiss, but conservative families may not want it printed. No source was found either way.

### Gaps
- No source found on photography rules inside Indian churches (flash bans, altar access, priest's rules), though these are often reported anecdotally.
- No Tamil or Andhra Christian specific source found (e.g. a thali/mangalsutra tied in church, nalugu-like functions).
- No India source confirms how common "family groups on the church steps" are, though the studios list family groups after the service.

## Parsi weddings: madav saro, adarni, supra nu murat, lagan (ara antar, haathevaro, chero bandhvanu), achoo michoo, lagan nu bhonu

### Takeaway
Order: madav saro (tree planting, about 4 days before) → adarni (groom's family brings gifts to the bride) → supra nu murat (married women, like a haldi) → nahan (ritual bath, wedding day) → achoo michoo (warding off the evil eye, at the door) → lagan in the evening (about 6:40 pm) in a baug or agiary: ara antar (cloth held between the couple, priests circle them seven times, the couple throw rice over the cloth), chero bandhvanu / haathevaro (hands and chairs tied with seven strands), ashirvad (two priests bless them, rice showered), dahi kumro (sharing from one plate) → lagan nu bhonu feast on banana leaves. Non-Parsis are generally not allowed inside an agiary, and in at least one wedding non-Parsis could not step on the main stage, so photographers shoot from a distance.

### Cited Findings
- Madav saro: a young tree (often mango) planted in a pot by each family, about four days before the lagan, with prayers by the family priest. — [CulturalIndia: Parsi](https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/regional-weddings/parsi-wedding.html); [bhagyavivah](https://blog.bhagyavivah.com/matrimonials/parsi-wedding-rituals/)
- Adarni: the groom's family visits the bride's home with gifts (clothes, jewellery). One source puts it on the third day before the lagan. — [Today's Traveller](https://www.todaystraveller.net/a-journey-through-parsi-wedding-rituals/); [CulturalIndia](https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/regional-weddings/parsi-wedding.html)
- Supra nu murat: the Parsi version of the haldi. Married women (four or five, sources disagree) are each given a supra (winnowing tray) with paan, supari, haldi, dates and coconut. — [Bollywood Shaadis: Parsi](https://www.bollywoodshaadis.com/articles/sacred-rituals-of-an-indian-parsi-wedding-will-leave-you-mesmerised-5560); [Today's Traveller](https://www.todaystraveller.net/a-journey-through-parsi-wedding-rituals/)
- Achoo michoo: an evil-eye ritual done by both sides before the wedding rites begin. — [CulturalIndia](https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/regional-weddings/parsi-wedding.html)
- Lagan timing and venue: most lagans start at about 6:40 pm (after sunset is auspicious), in a baug (garden or hall) or an agiary (fire temple). — [Today's Traveller](https://www.todaystraveller.net/a-journey-through-parsi-wedding-rituals/); [Marry Me Weddings](https://www.marrymeweddings.in/wpblog/?p=2388)
- Ara antar: bride and groom sit facing each other while two relatives hold a thick sheet between them. The priests circle them seven times, and at the end of the seventh round the couple throw rice over the sheet at each other. — [Today's Traveller](https://www.todaystraveller.net/a-journey-through-parsi-wedding-rituals/); [Bollywood Shaadis](https://www.bollywoodshaadis.com/articles/sacred-rituals-of-an-indian-parsi-wedding-will-leave-you-mesmerised-5560)
- Chero bandhvanu: the couple now sit side by side, tied with seven strands of string; called the most important part of the lagan. Haathevaro (hand joining): two priests officiate and put rice on the couple's open hands. — [Today's Traveller](https://www.todaystraveller.net/a-journey-through-parsi-wedding-rituals/); [bhagyavivah](https://blog.bhagyavivah.com/matrimonials/parsi-wedding-rituals/)
- Ashirvad: about an hour of priestly blessing in Persian and Avestan, with rice showered on the couple. A photographer's site calls the ashirvad seating (the couple facing each other) a signature Parsi moment. — [Today's Traveller](https://www.todaystraveller.net/a-journey-through-parsi-wedding-rituals/); [Girish Lone Photography](https://www.girishlone.com/behram--dhun.html)
- Dahi koomro: eating from one plate, a sign of shared life. — [Today's Traveller](https://www.todaystraveller.net/a-journey-through-parsi-wedding-rituals/)
- Lagan nu bhonu: a traditional feast for relatives, neighbours and friends, including sev and dahi, eggs and bananas. A guest's account describes the meal served on banana leaves. — [CulturalIndia](https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/regional-weddings/parsi-wedding.html); [Finely Chopped](https://finelychoppedk.wordpress.com/2010/01/24/and-they-ate-happily-ever-after-a-bengalis-take-on-the-parsi-laganu-bhonu-wedding-feast/)
- Photography access: non-Parsis are generally not allowed inside an agiary in India. At one wedding, non-Parsis "were not allowed to step on the main stage", so the photographer worked from a distance. Baugs, where everyone is welcome, are easier. — [Marry Me Weddings](https://www.marrymeweddings.in/wpblog/?p=2388); [Girish Lone Photography](https://www.girishlone.com/behram--dhun.html)
- The nahan bath, achoo michoo and the ashirvad seating are named as common photo highlights. — [Girish Lone Photography](https://www.girishlone.com/behram--dhun.html)

### Inferences
- Lagan photos are evening, low-light, often long-lens from a distance (non-Parsi photographer). Faces will be small and less sharp, so "judge against its own event" applies strongly.
- The ara antar is a key "first look" moment, but the faces are hidden by the cloth: the shot is the cloth held up, the priests, and the rice in the air. A "face hidden" rule must not punish it.
- During the ashirvad the couple sit still for about an hour, so there will be many near-identical frames. Collapsing them to the best one or two is right.
- The lagan nu bhonu feast photos are guests at long tables. They work as a few atmosphere or detail shots, not many.

### Gaps
- No Parsi-specific source on album structure or what Parsi couples ask photographers to print.
- "Adarni" detail is thin. "Supra nu murat" counts disagree (four or five women).
- No source found on whether cameras are allowed inside a particular agiary (it depends on its trustees).

## Typical album structure, posed vs candid, family groups, and how religious rules shape what is photographed

### Takeaway
No India-specific source on album page structure for these three communities was found. The studio shot lists suggest the same shape as other Indian albums: pre-wedding function(s) → the ceremony in fixed ritual order → couple portraits → family groups → the reception/feast. Photographers stress candids plus a planned list of family groups made with the couple. Religious rules decide where the photographer may stand (purdah areas, agiary interiors, church altar), and who may see the photos (social sharing, women-area photos).

### Inferences
- Per community, the planner's "big moments" map naturally to:
  - Muslim: groom's arrival/baraat; nikah (groom with qazi, signing, and the bride in her own room); first look (mirror/veil); couple with both families; joota chupai fun; rukhsati tears; walima stage portraits.
  - Christian: getting ready / father's blessing; aisle walk; vows/rings; minnu/manthrakodi or crowning (Kerala); register; church exit and family groups; cake/toast/dance.
  - Parsi: madav saro, supra nu murat women, achoo michoo at the door; ara antar cloth and rice; tied hands; ashirvad; feast.
- The app's current rule "the couple are often together" is weaker for Muslim nikah events (couple apart) and Parsi ara antar (faces hidden). The "most photographed two people" approach may still work across the whole folder, because couple portraits and the reception make up for it.
- No ritual labels in the app (owner's decision) is still fine: these moments are dense bursts of shooting, which the planner already finds.
