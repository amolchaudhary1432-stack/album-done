# Train small heads, borrow few models

The model that would help Album Done's brain most is not on any download page. It is a set of small scorers the project trains itself, on top of the SigLIP 2 B/32 image fingerprint it already plans and the 52 MediaPipe blendshape scores it already computes. Beyond that, only **three borrowed models** are both licence-clean for a paid app and worth their cost on a Galaxy A15: **MediaPipe Pose Landmarker** (Apache 2.0, 3-6 MB) for blessing, touching feet, hugging and garlanding; **MediaPipe Hand Landmarker** (Apache 2.0) for mehendi details and hands meeting; and, optionally, a **segmentation model** (MediaPipe multiclass or U2-Net, both Apache) for judging no-face detail shots. Almost every famous specialist model fails the licence test. The weights for emotion (AffectNet, RAF-DB), age (UTKFace, InsightFace, MiVOLO), gaze (Gaze360) and aesthetics (pyiqa, CLIP-IQA, Q-Align) are research-only, or were trained on data whose terms say research-only, even when the code is MIT or Apache. Google, Apple and Samsung all ship the same design: quick attribute models feed a small learned scorer trained on "A or B?" judgements, plus near-duplicate grouping and people importance. Album Done already has most of the attributes and is missing the learned scorer. Cheap models run on every photo, heavier ones only on a short list, and a set-level planner picks the album. With that cascade, the stack fits in an estimated **40-45 minutes for 2,500 photos** and adds about **100 MB**. A small student trained on Claude's pairwise judgements, anchored by real couples' albums, can plausibly beat Claude as a teacher on this narrow task, because it sees the whole folder and the couple's actual choices. No study has shown this for wedding albums, and no new model has yet been timed on the A15's Helio G99.

## The licence wall removes most famous models before speed matters

The trap is the gap between code licence and weight licence. pyiqa, the standard toolbox that hosts NIMA, MUSIQ, TOPIQ, LIQE, CLIP-IQA and Q-Align, is licensed under **PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 and the NTU S-Lab licence**, even though its GitHub sidebar says MIT ([IQA-PyTorch](https://github.com/chaofengc/IQA-PyTorch)). S-Lab allows use "for non-commercial purpose" only and tells commercial users to contact the authors ([CLIP-IQA LICENSE](https://github.com/IceClear/CLIP-IQA/blob/v2-3.8/LICENSE)). The HSEmotion expression library is Apache 2.0 and says "there is no limitation for both academic and commercial usage" of the code ([EmotiEffLib](https://github.com/sb-ai-lab/EmotiEffLib)). Its weights, however, are trained on AffectNet, whose academic licence (seen only in a third-party copy) restricts the dataset to non-commercial research and requires a separate agreement for companies ([AffectNet agreement copy](https://studylib.net/doc/27680111/affectnet-agreement-v2-30mar2023)). InsightFace is explicit: "the training data containing the annotation (and the models trained with these data) are available for non-commercial research purposes only" ([InsightFace](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface)). L2CS-Net gaze code is MIT ([L2CS-Net](https://github.com/Ahmednull/L2CS-Net/blob/main/LICENSE)), but its best weights are trained on Gaze360, whose dataset and code are non-commercial research only ([Gaze360](https://github.com/erkil1452/gaze360)). No source settles whether permissively licensed weights escape the terms of the data they were trained on. For a paid app sold to couples, the safe reading is that they do not.

That leaves three kinds of shippable model. The first is Google's MediaPipe models, whose model cards themselves say "LICENSED UNDER Apache License, Version 2.0" ([BlazePose card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20BlazePose%20GHUM%203D.pdf); [Hand Tracking card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20Hand%20Tracking%20(Lite_Full)%20with%20Fairness%20Oct%202021.pdf)). The second is a short list of Apache or MIT models whose training data is less contested: SigLIP 2, MobileSAM, MMPose's RTMPose and Florence-2. The third, and the cleanest of all, is heads the project trains on its own labels: Claude's pairwise picks on the harvested Indian wedding sets and the couple's real album for wedding-1. That third category runs through every row of the next section.

### Laughter and crying faces come from blendshapes; tears need context

Every strong small expression model found sits on AffectNet or RAF-DB. HSEmotion is the fastest and best documented. Its mobilenet_7 reaches **64.71% on AffectNet's 7 classes at 16 ± 5 ms per face and 14 MB** on a Samsung Fold 3, and enet_b0_8_va_mtl adds valence and arousal at 60 ms ([EmotiEffLib](https://github.com/sb-ai-lab/EmotiEffLib)). OpenCV Zoo's Progressive Teacher model is built to run after YuNet and reports 88.27% on RAF-DB, but its folder has no licence file ([OpenCV Zoo FER](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/tree/main/models/facial_expression_recognition)). EmoNet is plainly out under CC BY-NC-ND ([EmoNet](https://github.com/face-analysis/emonet/blob/master/LICENSE.txt)). FER+ in the ONNX Model Zoo is MIT, a 64x64 greyscale input with 8 classes ([ONNX Model Zoo](https://github.com/onnx/models/tree/main/validated/vision/body_analysis/emotion_ferplus)). It is old, and it was trained on FER2013 images scraped from search results.

None of these is much better than what the app's own blendshapes can do. One reviewed study reports about **71% on FER2013 from MediaPipe blendshape features** ([Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/emotion-estimation-from-video-footage-with-lstm)). An unreviewed random forest on the 52 scores gets 51.36%, against a stated human baseline of 65-68% ([mirror](https://cloudfare-proxy.pages.dev/Nonovogo/face-emotions2)). Both figures are secondhand, but they put a blendshape classifier in the same range as the image models. It also costs nothing extra at run time, and it is trained on the app's own labelled wedding faces, so no dataset question arises. The categories the album needs map onto blendshapes directly. Laughing is smile plus jawOpen plus cheek and eye squint, which separates it from mid-talk (jawOpen without smile). A crying face is brow-inner-up plus mouth-frown. Serious ritual faces are "neutral", and the planner already forgives them in rites. At 60-66% accuracy for even the best image models, expression has to be a soft bonus, never a hard reject.

Tears themselves (wet eyes, a tissue, a hand over the mouth) are labelled by no model found. The workable signal is a combination: a sad-face blendshape pattern, a SigLIP 2 photo-level prompt such as "a woman wiping tears with a tissue" (accuracy untested), and context, since the photo falls in the last dense burst of the main event, near the bride, with an elder in frame. The project's own notes already list "no bonus for tears and laughter" as a known gap ([HOW_IT_PICKS.md](../../HOW_IT_PICKS.md)). This route fixes that gap without a new download.

### Elders need a coarse age band per person, not a model per face

The accurate age models cannot ship. MiVOLO reaches an age error of about 4.2 years on IMDB-clean and can use the body when the face is small or turned ([MiVOLO](https://github.com/WildChlamydia/MiVOLO)). But MiVOLO v2 is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([mivolo_v2](https://huggingface.co/iitolstykh/mivolo_v2)), and v1's custom licence PDF could not be read. An Apache-labelled ViT age-gender model reports a 4.5-year error, but it is an **86.8M-parameter ViT trained on UTKFace** ([onnx-community](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/age-gender-prediction-ONNX)), a dataset commonly described as research-only (not verified here). **FairFace** is the one well-known age resource with an "Indian" group and a permissive-looking licence (CC BY 4.0). It has 108,501 faces balanced across seven race groups, and its authors report models more consistent across groups ([arXiv 1908.04913](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04913); [FairFace repo](https://github.com/joojs/fairface)). Its 9 age bins top out at about 60% top-1 ([hyper.ai](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/1908.04913)), but "60+ against under 40" is a far easier call.

The practical design is to collapse FairFace to child, adult and 60+, train a tiny head, and run it once per person cluster on that person's best face crop, not on every face. A 2,500-photo wedding has a few hundred clusters, so even a 0.3 s SigLIP pass on each crop costs a minute or two. Which features the head should use, a SigLIP 2 face-crop embedding or the existing SFace fingerprint, is open. SFace is trained to recognise a person across ages, so it may carry little age signal, and this has to be measured. Age then ranks rather than decides. It combines with the stronger existing signal, "photographed beside the couple at several times of day", and with the earlier research's rule that an elderly face seen even once beside the couple must never be dropped. Wedding make-up, stage light and dyed hair will push estimates around, and no source gives per-group error for South Asian faces. FairFace's Flickr images carry their own licences, which needs a lawyer's look before shipping.

### Mutual gaze is geometry the app can already compute

No small, permissively trained gaze model was found. L2CS-Net uses a ResNet-50 and the non-commercial Gaze360 data. 6DRepNet (MIT, [licence](https://github.com/thohemp/6DRepNet/blob/master/LICENSE)) would only repeat the head angle the Face Landmarker transform already gives. "The couple looking at each other", the candid bonus in the product scope, can be a rule instead: A's head yaw points towards B's side and B's towards A's, the faces are at roughly the same height, both sets of eyes are open, and the eyeLookIn/Out blendshapes agree with the head direction. It needs no model and no licence, and it can be unit-tested in pure Kotlin like the rest of the planner.

### Pose and hands are the two borrowed models that unlock new moments

Blessing, touching feet (pranam), hugging, garlanding and dancing are what family albums are made of, and nothing in the current stack sees bodies beyond a box. **MediaPipe Pose Landmarker** is the cleanest fit. Its model card states Apache 2.0, and it ships Lite (3 MB), Full (6 MB) and Heavy (26 MB) variants with 33 landmarks from a 256x256 person crop. In the card's fairness table, Southern Asia scores **86.6 / 91.2 / 96.2** (Lite / Full / Heavy) against averages of 87.0 / 91.8 / 94.2 ([BlazePose card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20BlazePose%20GHUM%203D.pdf)). It runs at 20-53 ms on a Pixel 3 GPU ([MediaPipe docs](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/docs/solutions/pose.md)). It runs in the same MediaPipe runtime the app already bundles. It is a single-person model, though, so it has to run once per person box, and the old docs assume people 2-4 m from the camera.

For crowded stages, MMPose offers RTMPose-s (5.47M parameters, COCO AP 72.2, **13.89 ms on a Snapdragon 865** with ncnn) and the one-pass multi-person RTMO-s ([RTMPose](https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmpose/tree/main/projects/rtmpose); [RTMO](https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmpose/tree/main/projects/rtmo)). MMPose's code is Apache 2.0, but its published checkpoints are trained on AI Challenger plus COCO, and AI Challenger's terms were not verified. MoveNet MultiPose stops at 6 people ([MoveNet](https://github.com/tensorflow/tfjs-models/blob/master/pose-detection/src/movenet/README.md)). Since the gestures that matter happen between the couple and one or two others, MediaPipe Pose on the couple's boxes and their nearest neighbours is enough. It should run only on short-listed photos.

No model labels "blessing" or "pranam". These come from rules on keypoints across two skeletons. A blessing is one person's wrist within a head-width above another's head. Touching feet is a bent or kneeling torso with wrists near another person's ankles. A hug is overlapping shoulders with wrists behind the other's back. A garland is both arms raised with wrists near the other's neck. No published accuracy exists for any of these, and lehengas and sarees hide hips and knees, so each rule needs testing on real wedding photos. The rules stay internal: the owner has ruled out ritual labels on screen ([CLAUDE.md](../../../CLAUDE.md)). The research on human-object interaction confirms there is no phone-ready shortcut. UnionDet and its successors are GPU-class and built on research-only benchmarks ([UnionDet](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/html/2391_ECCV_2020_paper.php)).

**MediaPipe Hand Landmarker** is the natural partner. It is Apache 2.0, runs a palm detector (95.7% average precision) and returns 21 3D landmarks on a 224x224 crop, and it was evaluated across 14 regions ([Hand card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20Hand%20Tracking%20(Lite_Full)%20with%20Fairness%20Oct%202021.pdf); [docs](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/docs/solutions/hands.md)). It does two jobs. On no-face photos, one to four large hands filling the frame is a strong "mehendi detail" signal for the roughly 10% detail quota. On couple photos, the couple's hands meeting between them points to the ring, the garland or joined hands. Dark henna patterns may lower its confidence (untested), and no Helio G99 timing exists.

### Composition comes from boxes; a mask earns its place only for detail shots

Rule-of-thirds placement, cramped framing and subject cut-off can all be computed from the YuNet and EfficientDet boxes the app already has. A pixel mask adds real value in one place: telling a meaningful no-face detail (jewellery, henna, a ritual object) from clutter or an empty chair. The project hit exactly this failure when backs of guests were picked as "detail shots" ([HOW_IT_PICKS.md](../../HOW_IT_PICKS.md)). The cheapest mask is **MediaPipe's multiclass segmenter**: 256x256 input, six classes (hair, body skin, face skin, clothes, accessories, background), Apache 2.0 per a mirrored model card ([MediaPipe segmenter](https://developers.google.cn/mediapipe/solutions/vision/image_segmenter); [card mirror](https://dcmpx.remotevs.com/com/googleapis/storage/SL/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20Multiclass%20Segmentation.pdf)). It was trained for selfies, so it is weak on wide stage shots. U2-Net is Apache 2.0, but only according to an ONNX repackaging ([Heliosoph](https://huggingface.co/Heliosoph/u2net-onnx)). MobileSAM is Apache 2.0, with 9.66M parameters and ~12 ms on a GPU, but takes "around 3 s" on a Mac i5 CPU and needs a prompt ([MobileSAM](https://github.com/ChaoningZhang/MobileSAM)). RMBG-2.0 is non-commercial, and commercial use requires a BRIA agreement ([RMBG-2.0](https://huggingface.co/briaai/RMBG-2.0/blob/refs%2Fpr%2F6/README.md)). SigLIP 2 prompts such as "a close-up of hands with henna" or "a cluttered photo" already cover much of the detail judgement, so a segmenter is the last addition, not the first. No licensed, phone-ready cropping scorer (GAIC) was verified.

### Aesthetic weights are either non-commercial or trained on other people's taste

NIMA (idealo, MobileNet, Apache 2.0 code) reaches **SRCC 0.609 on AVA** for aesthetics and 0.675 on TID2013 for technical quality, and the repository was archived in December 2024 ([idealo](https://github.com/idealo/image-quality-assessment)). MANIQA's Apache code reaches a strong KonIQ SRCC of 0.930, but it uses a ViT plus Swin blocks trained on KonIQ, KADID and PIPAL ([MANIQA](https://github.com/IIGROUP/MANIQA)). MUSIQ's checkpoints sit in Google Research's monorepo with no licence file of their own ([MUSIQ](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/musiq)). The LAION v2 predictor is a "simple neural net" on CLIP ViT-L/14 embeddings with no stated weight licence ([improved-aesthetic-predictor](https://github.com/christophschuhmann/improved-aesthetic-predictor)). The SigLIP-based aesthetic predictor v2.5 is AGPL-3.0, a strong copyleft licence that would pull the app's source into its terms ([ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fdiscus0434%2Faesthetic-predictor-v2-5)). AVA's images come from DPChallenge, and KonIQ-10k is "freely available to the research community", which leaves commercial use unaddressed ([KonIQ mirror](https://backiee.wasmer.app/http_database_mmsp-kn_de/koniq-10k-database_html)).

Even with clean licences, these models would answer the wrong question. AVA measures photo-contest taste. KonIQ and SPAQ measure technical distortion, which the app already covers with face-crop sharpness, exposure and blur rules. The LAION and SILVA designs, a small head on frozen CLIP or SigLIP embeddings ([SILVA](https://github.com/jannchie/silva)), show the right shape. Train that same shape on SigLIP 2 B/32 with the project's own pairwise labels, and the result scores album-worthiness instead of contest appeal, with no licence question at all. The CLIP-IQA idea of contrasting prompt pairs ("a sharp, well-lit photo" against "a blurry, dark photo") can be re-implemented from scratch: S-Lab covers its code and weights, not the idea.

### Near-duplicates and scenes need no second backbone

Wedding bursts are nearly pixel-identical, so a 64-bit perceptual hash on a 32x32 thumbnail plus the time gap catches most of them at almost no cost. The hard case is "same family, same stage, slightly different frame". SigLIP 2 cosine similarity combined with SFace identity sets (same people) should handle it without merging different families against one backdrop. Self-supervised DINO features look stronger for exact matching. In one CAD-render test DINOv2's median correct-match rank was 1 against CLIP's 6 ([VideoCAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24838)), and an ImageNet audit switched to DINOv2 because CLIP duplicates "were not satisfactory" ([arXiv 2412.00076](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.00076)). Neither result is a wedding-scale benchmark. DINOv3 ships under a custom Meta licence with a gated download. It must travel with a copy of the agreement, bans reverse engineering, carries an indemnity, and lets Meta change the terms at will ([DINOv3 LICENSE](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). DINOv2-S (Apache 2.0 by training knowledge, unverified) is the fallback, but only if tests on wedding-1 show SigLIP mis-grouping. Pooling SigLIP's own patch tokens into a grid descriptor is an untested middle path.

For scenes, Places365's CNNs are CC BY (attribution, commercial use fine), but they are old ResNet and VGG classifiers at about **54.7% top-1** with no mobile variant ([Places365](https://github.com/CSAILVision/places365)). Events already come from the photographer's folders, and SigLIP 2 prompts can name "people dancing" or "food buffet" directly. The project's own test also showed the limits of naming rituals. A classifier on SigLIP 2 features fell from 81% to **59%** once whole weddings were kept apart, which is why the owner parked ritual sorting ([HOW_IT_PICKS.md](../../HOW_IT_PICKS.md)).

### Captions are a nice-to-have that the scorer can mostly replace

**Florence-2-base** is the smallest commercially clean captioner and detector: 0.23B parameters, MIT, with an official ONNX port ([onnx-community](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/Florence-2-base/blob/main/README.md)) and a community INT8 export of ~270 MB that keeps captioning intact while losing some small-text OCR ([Heliosoph INT8](https://huggingface.co/Heliosoph/florence-2-base-ft-quantized-onnx)). It describes and grounds, but it cannot reason ("is anyone mid-blink?"). Moondream 3 is Business Source License 1.1: internal commercial use is allowed, but offering it to third parties needs a deal, which makes an app sold to couples risky ([moondream3 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/moondream/moondream3-preview/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). The earlier research found the Moondream repository itself Apache 2.0 ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vikhyat/moondream/main/LICENSE)), SmolVLM Apache 2.0 ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/smolvlm)), and Qwen3-VL-2B Apache only according to mirrors ([mirror](https://huggingface.co/LibreYOLO/LibreGroundQwen3VL2b)). At an estimated 5-20 s per photo on the G99, any of them can only touch a short list. The bigger point is that the "why it is in the album" sentence can come from the scorer itself. If the learned head is readable (see below), the app can say "both of you looking at each other, Nani in frame, everyone's eyes open" from its own signals, which is truer than a caption. Pick at most one generative model, and only after the heads plateau.

## Google, Apple and Samsung ship attribute models plus a learned fuser

The big products all split the job the same way. **Google Top Shot** scored up to 90 frames around the shutter press on three groups of criteria: functional (lighting), objective ("are the subject's eyes open? are they smiling?") and subjective (emotional expressions such as amusement or surprise). A customised MobileNet spent its early layers on blur and its later compute on eyes and expressions. Faces were scored with a **Generalized Additive Model (GAM)**, and exposure time, gyroscope and optical flow fed the final quality score ([Google Research](https://www.research.google/blog/top-shot-on-pixel-3/); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/google-pixel-3-top-shot-blog-936664)). A GAM adds up one readable curve per signal, so it shows how each signal moved the score. **Google Clips** found raters "very consistent" on pairwise comparisons and less consistent on direct scores. It collected over **50 million pairwise comparisons**, had server models label a large photo set, distilled them into an on-device MobileNet, and mapped that model's content outputs to one score trained on the pairs ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/automatic-photography-with-google-clips/)). **Best Take** finds photos taken in sequence by timestamp and picks the best face per person from similar frames. Auto Best Take on the Pixel 10 recommends better photos of "the faces you capture often", which is a people-importance signal ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/photos/how-google-photos-best-take-works/); [Google Store](https://store.google.com/magazine/pixel-best-take?hl=en-US)). **Photo Stacks** groups similar photos taken close together and picks a top pick the user can change. Google says about a third of most galleries are similar photos ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/photos/google-photos-organization-updates-november-2023/)), but has published nothing about the model behind it.

**Apple** runs one multi-task scene network (ANSA) whose heads feed Memories and Featured Photos: scene tags for a knowledge graph, quality ranking for cover photos, and a filter for "utility" content such as receipts ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/on-device-scene-analysis)). The public Vision API shows the fused score directly. `overallScore` "incorporates aesthetic score, failure score, and utility labels", and `isUtility` marks images "not necessarily of poor image quality" that lack memorable content ([Apple developer docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/imageaestheticsscoresobservation.md)). Memories' key photos also use a population prior, "iconic scenes" learned across devices with differential privacy ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/scenes-differential-privacy)). People are clustered from face and upper-body features, so people turned away still count ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/recognizing-people-photos)). **Samsung** has published almost nothing. Single Take captures up to 14 photos and videos and shows an "AI Best Moments" pick ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/ca/support/mobile-devices/galaxy-s21-single-take)), and that is the whole public record.

Three lessons transfer to Album Done. First, Apple's aesthetic / failure / utility split is a ready-made shape for the scorer. Failure is blinks, blur and cut-offs. Utility is fine but not album-worthy: WhatsApp forwards, screenshots and invitation cards that end up in the folder. Second, a readable GAM-style head (Top Shot) fits the viewer's "why it is in the album" line better than an opaque network. Third, Apple's population prior needs a server, so the offline equivalent is a prior built once from the project's labelled weddings and shipped inside the APK.

## Cascade, stack, then pick the set

Album Done is already a cascade. YuNet runs on every photo, the mesh model reads each face crop, SFace fingerprints faces, and the planner chooses the set. The evidence says to keep extending that shape rather than stacking big models on every photo. Google's "Wisdom of Committees" found **cascades beat single models at every compute budget**, while ensembles helped mainly at large budgets. Two EfficientNet-B5s matched B7 with almost 50% fewer FLOPs, and one cascade was 5.4x faster than B7 at equal accuracy ([arXiv 2012.01988](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.01988); [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/model-ensembles-are-faster-than-you-think/)). Those numbers come from ImageNet, not photo curation, but the logic holds. In tier 1, cheap signals run on all 2,500 photos: today's face stack, a perceptual hash and the SigLIP 2 embedding. In tier 2, pose, hands and age run only on the short list, roughly the top 20-30% of each moment plus borderline photos and one best face per person. In tier 3, the planner picks.

Fusion should be **stacking with one small learned head** in the Clips pattern. Its inputs are the face signals (worst-face eyes, gaze, expression head outputs, sharpness, exposure), photo signals (blur, tilt, people boxes) and the SigLIP 2 embedding. It is trained on pairwise "A beats B within the same moment" labels, because same-moment pairs hold the content fixed and isolate "which frame". Microsoft's burst picker took exactly that route: 838,038 within-burst pairs gave a 0.47 MB model that matched the user's favourite frame **64.1%** of the time (86.2% top-3) ([arXiv 1803.07212](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07212)). The planner's existing rules (people first, moments, variety, no repeated pose) work as a hand-tuned set objective. Set-level learning has a published path. Tschiatschek and colleagues learned the weights of a mix of submodular terms (coverage, diversity, quality) from human image-collection summaries and beat all competitors on their 14-collection dataset ([NeurIPS 2014](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2014/hash/41321d693c015a6a92f55f29c8a76079-Abstract.html)). A sequential DPP respects time order while keeping each photo's quality score separate from its similarity features ([NeurIPS 2014](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2014/hash/0eec27c419d0fe24e53c90338cdc8bc6-Abstract.html)). Whole-set models barely beat simple diversity baselines, though: 45.5% against 43.75% precision in one album study ([Yu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.02977)). So the best plan is to keep the planner greedy and testable and, later, learn its few weights from real albums rather than replace it.

The time budget comes from the project's own measurement: 2,500 photos in 25 min 48 s on the A15 ([CLAUDE.md](../../../CLAUDE.md)). That leaves about **0.10 s extra per photo** for a 30-minute target, 0.46 s for 45 minutes and **0.82 s for 60 minutes**. Every per-model time below is an estimate scaled from other chips, because no new model has been timed on a G99.

| Stage | Runs on | Estimated added time (2,500 photos) | Added app size |
|---|---|---|---|

## A small student can beat its teacher here, if gold labels anchor it

The plan is to have Claude act as the teacher on the build machine and distil its pairwise judgements into heads on the phone. Distillation research supports the idea that a narrow student can outdo a broad teacher. **Distilling step-by-step** trained a 770M T5 on a 540B PaLM's rationales and labels, and the student beat few-shot PaLM using only 80% of the data, where plain fine-tuning of the same T5 could not match PaLM even with all of it ([arXiv 2305.02301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02301)). **Noisy Student** trained an equal-or-larger student on teacher pseudo-labels with dropout, stochastic depth and RandAugment. It reached 88.4% on ImageNet and lifted ImageNet-A from 61.0% to 83.7%, and the authors credit the noise for the student beating its teacher ([CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Xie_Self-Training_With_Noisy_Student_Improves_ImageNet_Classification_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)). Distillation works best with a fixed teacher, the same augmented views for teacher and student, and long training ([Beyer et al., CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Beyer_Knowledge_Distillation_A_Good_Teacher_Is_Patient_and_Consistent_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)). In a narrow industrial task, waste sorting, compact students distilled from large VLMs ran faster, held steady when prompts changed and were more accurate than the teacher ([ScienceDirect 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921344926001096)). One multilingual study found students below the teacher on retrieval but able to beat it on consistency tasks such as ranking ([arXiv 2510.26271](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26271v1)), and album picking is a ranking task.

There are three reasons a student could beat Claude at matching a couple's album. It sees what a pairwise teacher does not: the whole folder, burst neighbours, how often each person appears, and the time order. It is trained on exactly one task. And averaging thousands of noisy teacher pairs (calibrated with Elo or Bradley-Terry, as the earlier research proposed) smooths out the teacher's one-off mistakes. The limits are just as clear. Plain pseudo-labels hurt when they are wrong ([arXiv 2502.12584](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12584v2)), and GPT-4V labels were too noisy for a ViT-Small to fit even with 4,000 human labels added ([arXiv 2402.13232](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13232)). One medical-imaging preprint found a gold-label-only classifier passed its zero-shot labeller at **about 100 gold labels**, after which weak labels mostly added contamination ([arXiv 2605.24771](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24771.pdf)). That comes from a different field, so it is indicative only, but it says the matched photos from real couples' albums (AlbumScore) are worth far more per example than more Claude pairs. Keep the two label sources separate and weighted, and judge every head only against real albums. No study has distilled VLM album-worthiness judgements into a mobile model with published numbers. A student "beating the teacher" here is a plausible hypothesis to test on wedding-1, not a result anyone has shown.

The same machinery gives offline personalisation for free. Every swap or Remove in the viewer is a pairwise preference ("this one, not the one it replaced"). Those pairs can nudge the fusion head for that album only, on the phone, with a cap on how far it moves from the shipped weights, in the spirit of few-shot personal-aesthetics work ([arXiv 2407.07176](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07176v1)). The re-made album then reflects the couple's own taste. Federated learning, as used for Gboard ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/advances-in-private-training-for-production-on-device-language-models/)), needs a server and would break the no-internet promise.

## Conclusion

The research turns "which models should we add?" into "which labels should we collect?". The open-model world is rich in emotion, age, gaze and aesthetic networks, and nearly all of them carry somebody else's research-only data. The models that are clean and phone-sized (SigLIP 2 and MediaPipe Pose, Hands and Face) are generic perception, and they become album judgement only through heads trained on Indian wedding choices. That makes the project's real moat the labelled pairs: Claude's within-moment judgements, the couples' matched albums, and later each bride's own swaps. These are also what decide whether a student beats its teacher. The models are commodity parts any competitor can download.

Two practical consequences follow. First, the single A15 measurement of SigLIP 2 B/32 int8 still gates almost everything, since heads 1, 2 and 5 all ride on it. If it is slow, the fallback is a smaller encoder or a short-list pass, not more specialist models. Second, the app's "why it's in the album" line should be built from a readable scorer, as Top Shot did with its GAM, rather than from a captioning model. That gives the couple honest reasons, keeps the app small, and probably removes the strongest remaining reason to ship a talking model at all.
